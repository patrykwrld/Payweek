# Getting Payweek in front of people

Written as a plan you can execute on a phone between shifts, not a marketing
deck. Day 1 is the next day you sit down with it.

---

## The fact that decides everything else

**Nobody can install Payweek right now.** It is on a closed track, so the only
thing a stranger can do with a link is put their email on the waitlist. That
single constraint should shape every post you make this fortnight, because the
usual advice — drive traffic, get installs — does not apply to an app that
cannot be installed.

So there are two lanes, and they are not equally urgent.

**Lane A — end the blocker.** Twelve Android testers, opted in, for fourteen
*continuous* days. Until that is satisfied nothing else you do can turn into a
launch. Every day without twelve is a day the clock has not started.

**Lane B — build the list.** Everyone else goes on the waitlist so that launch
day starts with an audience instead of silence.

Lane A is worth more per person by a wide margin. One Android tester who opts
in this week is worth more than fifty waitlist emails, because the testers are
literally the thing standing between you and a public listing. Post
accordingly: your Day 1 asks are for **testers**, and the waitlist catches
everybody who cannot help.

A useful reframe for Lane A, because asking for favours is uncomfortable: you
are not asking anyone to use an app. You are asking them to tap a link and tap
"Become a tester". Play counts opt-ins, not usage. That is a thirty-second
favour, and it is honest to describe it that way.

## Where these people actually are

Ranked by how much of your limited time each deserves. This ordering is
specific to *this* audience — UK agency workers, mostly Android, working
shifts — and would be wrong for almost any other product.

### 1. Facebook groups — do this first, every day

This is where UK warehouse and agency workers genuinely congregate, and it is
the only channel that can realistically produce twelve testers in a week.
The groups worth finding:

- **Site-specific.** Groups for individual warehouses and distribution centres
  — search the site name, the postcode, the estate. These are the highest
  intent places on the internet for this product.
- **Agency-specific.** Most large agencies have unofficial worker groups where
  people compare shifts and complain about pay. Search the agency names you
  have worked for.
- **Town and city jobs groups.** "Jobs in <town>", "<town> Work & Jobs".
  Bigger, noisier, lower intent, but they are where new agency workers land.
- **Shift-pattern groups.** Nights, 4-on-4-off. Smaller, very engaged.

Join five to ten of these before you post anything. Most have a rule against
promotion and an admin who enforces it, so read the pinned rules first and
message the admin where a group looks strict. "I've built a free app for
agency workers, is it alright if I post it?" gets a yes surprisingly often,
and a no costs you nothing but saves you a ban.

### 2. TikTok — the only channel that can go wide

Short vertical video is where a product like this either catches or it does
not. The advantage you have is that the payoff is *visual and instant*: a
payslip, a number, and the app saying it is £30 short. That is a three-second
hook that needs no explanation.

Post daily. It costs nothing and the algorithm, not your follower count,
decides who sees it — which is the one place where being unknown is not a
disadvantage.

### 3. Reddit — one good post, not a campaign

r/UKJobs, r/AskUK, r/UKPersonalFinance, r/employment and the UK warehouse
subreddits. Reddit will destroy you for spamming and reward you for being
useful, so this is a place for one honest post, not a schedule.

The rule everyone quotes is 90/10 — ninety percent participating, ten percent
or less promoting — but the rule that actually matters is: **read that
subreddit's sidebar first, and disclose that it is yours.** Accounts that
exist mainly to promote get banned; accounts that take part and occasionally
mention their own work do not. Never use a second account to upvote or agree
with yourself; it is the single fastest way to get the domain banned sitewide.

I could not verify each subreddit's current promotion rules from here — they
change and they are set per community — so check the sidebar of each one on
the day you post. That is not a formality: several UK subs ban app links
outright and you would rather learn that from the sidebar than from a mod.

### 4. Instagram — free, because it is the same video

You asked about Instagram earlier. Worth doing only as a place to re-upload
the TikToks as Reels — same file, zero extra work. Do not write separate
content for it. As a discovery channel for this audience it is materially
worse than TikTok.

### 5. Twitter/X — skip it

You asked about this too, so here is the straight answer: your audience is not
there. UK agency and warehouse workers do not discuss shift pay on X, and a
new account with no following gets no distribution. Setting one up is an hour
you should spend in Facebook groups. Reserve the handle if you like the name,
post nothing, and revisit after launch.

## Day 1

About three hours, and it does not need to be one sitting.

**First 45 minutes — set the accounts up.**

- TikTok account: `payweek.app`. Bio: *Know what you're owed before payday.
  Free UK shift & pay tracker.* Link: `payweek.app/?s=tiktok`
- Instagram: same handle, same bio, link `payweek.app/?s=ig`
- Profile picture: `assets/play/icon-512.png`, already in this repo.
- Join 8–10 Facebook groups from the list above. Do not post yet. Several have
  a waiting period before you can, which is exactly why this is the first
  thing you do rather than the last.

**Next 45 minutes — film four videos in one go.**

Film them all in one sitting, post them across the week. Vertical, phone
camera, no editing app required, natural light, your actual voice. Polished
production would work *against* you here — it reads as an advert. Scripts are
in the content bank below.

**Next 30 minutes — the tester ask.**

Post the tester ask (copy below) in the two friendliest groups you joined, and
send it to any WhatsApp group you are in with people you work with. This is
the highest-value half hour of the day because it is the only one aimed at
Lane A.

**Last 30 minutes — the Reddit post.**

One post, to whichever of the UK job subs allows it. Not a link drop: the post
below is a genuine question with the app mentioned as context.

**Then stop.** Do not check the numbers. There will not be any yet, and
watching a waitlist that says zero on day one is how people quit in week two.

## Days 2–14

| Day | Lane A (testers) | Lane B (audience) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Follow up anyone who said yes but has not opted in | Post video 2 |
| 3 | Two more groups | Reply to every comment on Day 1's posts |
| 4 | — | Post video 3 |
| 5 | Chase again — this is where most opt-ins actually land | Reels re-upload of 1–3 |
| 6 | Count opt-ins in Play Console | Post video 4 |
| 7 | **Review**: how many of the twelve? | Read the signup sources (SQL below) |
| 8–14 | Top up to twelve and hold it | Repeat what worked, drop what did not |

The number that matters on Day 7 is not followers or views. It is **how many
of the twelve you have**, and whether the fourteen-day clock has started.

One warning about the fourteen days, because it is the thing that catches
people out: it is twelve testers *continuously*. If someone uninstalls on day
nine you can drop below twelve and the clock resets. Recruit fifteen or
sixteen so you have slack, and do not tell anyone they can uninstall when they
are done.

## The content bank

Written to be used, not adapted. Change the details to your own — they work
better in your words.

### Video 0 — the one that already exists

`scripts/film-demo.mjs` films a 1080×1920, 32-second walk through the real
app: the week total, a day tapped, a shift that runs past midnight split
across two rates, and the payslip check landing on "You're £30.00 short".
Captions are burned in. It ends on a card with the address.

Post it as-is, or talk over it — a screen recording with your own voice over
the top is the format that works here, and it saves you filming a screen with
a second phone. Re-run the script after any UI change and the advert matches
the app again.

### Video 1 — the hook that needs no explanation

> *[Phone showing a payslip]* "Right, so this is my payslip from last week.
> £598. Looks fine, doesn't it."
>
> *[Open the app]* "This is every shift I actually worked. Two nights, a
> Saturday. It comes to £628."
>
> *[The red line]* "So they're thirty quid short. And I'd never have known,
> because who's sat there working out night rates on a Sunday?"
>
> "It's free, it's called Payweek, link's in the bio."

### Video 2 — the one that gets argued about in the comments

> "Tell me if this is just me. You do a shift that starts at six in the
> evening and finishes at two in the morning. What are you actually getting
> paid?"
>
> "Because it's four hours at your day rate, four at nights — and half of
> them can't even work that out, never mind you."
>
> "That's the whole reason I built this."

Arguments in the comments are the point. Every reply pushes it further.

### Video 3 — holiday pay

> "Does anyone actually know what their holiday pay should be?"
>
> "12.07% of everything you earn. Every hour you work you're owed about seven
> minutes of paid holiday, and almost nobody checks whether they got it."

### Video 4 — the credibility one

> "445,000 jobs in this country were paid below minimum wage last year. 1.4
> million people didn't even get a payslip."
>
> "There's a new government body since April — the Fair Work Agency — that
> exists to enforce this. But you can't report what you can't prove, and you
> can't prove it if you haven't got your own record of the hours."

Those figures are real and worth stating accurately: an estimated **445,000
jobs paid below the minimum wage in 2025**, **1.4 million workers reporting no
payslip in 2023–24**, and **2.2 million jobs with no paid annual leave in
2025**. The **Fair Work Agency** was established on **7 April 2026** as the
single enforcement body for UK employment rights. Do not round these up or
dramatise them — the accuracy is the credibility.

## The rules that get you banned

Worth reading once, because one ban costs more than a week of posting gains.

- **Never post the same text into ten groups in ten minutes.** That is the
  exact pattern Facebook's spam detection looks for. Space them out, and
  change a sentence each time.
- **Do not use a second account to agree with yourself** anywhere. On Reddit
  it can get the payweek.app domain banned sitewide, which is unrecoverable.
- **Disclose that it is yours**, every time, on every platform. It costs you
  nothing — people respond well to "I built this" — and hiding it is what
  gets removed.
- **Do not buy followers or engagement.** For a product whose entire pitch is
  "no ads, no tracking, honest numbers", getting caught doing this is fatal in
  a way it would not be for a normal app.
- **Never claim Payweek calculates tax or is legal advice.** It estimates
  gross pay. The footer already says this and your posts must match it.

## How you will know if it is working

The landing page now records which channel each signup came from. Links go out
with a tag on the end:

| Where you post | Link to use |
| --- | --- |
| TikTok bio | `payweek.app/?s=tiktok` |
| Instagram bio | `payweek.app/?s=ig` |
| A Facebook group | `payweek.app/?s=fb-nights` (name the group) |
| Reddit | `payweek.app/?s=reddit` |
| WhatsApp | `payweek.app/?s=wa` |

Anything after `?s=` is lowercased and stripped to letters, numbers and
hyphens, so you cannot break it by typing something odd. No tag means the row
records `direct`.

Read the results in the Supabase SQL editor:

```sql
select coalesce(source, 'unknown') as came_from,
       count(*) as signups,
       max(created_at) as latest
from public.launch_signups
group by 1
order by signups desc;
```

This is deliberately first-party and nothing else: no analytics script, no
pixel, no third party. The page promises no tracking and that promise should
survive contact with marketing. What you get is the one number you actually
need — which channel produced a human who typed their email — and nothing
about anyone who did not.

**Expect small numbers.** Ten signups in week one from a standing start is a
normal result, not a failure. The metric that decides whether this fortnight
worked is the tester count, and twelve is a number you can reach by asking
about thirty people.
