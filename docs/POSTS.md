# The posts, and exactly how to use them

Four graphics, four captions, and the instructions for actually getting them
in front of people. `docs/GROWTH.md` is the strategy; this is the doing.

Regenerate the images after any brand change:

```sh
npx vite --port 5199
node scripts/make-posts.mjs
```

**Every figure in these is the app's own arithmetic**, from the same fixture
as the screenshots and the film. £50.63 + £60.75 = £111.38. £467.53 − £437.53
= £30.00. If you change a number in a caption, change it in the graphic too —
the first person who adds it up in the comments will be right and you will be
wrong.

# Part 2 — instructions

## Step 1 · Find the groups (30 minutes, once)

In the Facebook search bar, switch to the **Groups** tab and search:

- the name of every warehouse or site you have worked at
- the name of every agency you have worked for
- `warehouse jobs <your town>`
- `<your town> jobs`
- `agency work uk`
- `night shift uk`

Join **8 to 10**. Prioritise in this order: site-specific, then
agency-specific, then town jobs groups. A 400-member group for one
distribution centre is worth more than a 90,000-member national jobs group,
because the small one is full of people who all get paid by the same agency.

**Answering the membership questions.** Most groups ask one or two. Answer
them as a worker, because you are one — "I work agency at <site>" gets you in;
anything that reads like a business gets you rejected. Do not mention the app
at this stage.

## Step 2 · Read the rules before you post anything

Open each group, tap **About**, read the rules. You are looking for one thing:
whether promotion or links are banned. Three outcomes:

- **No rule against it** → post normally.
- **"No promotion" / "No selling"** → message an admin first. Literally: *"Hi
  — I work agency myself and I've made a free app for working out shift pay.
  No ads, nothing for sale. Is it alright if I post about it?"* You get a yes
  more often than you would think, and a no costs you nothing but saves the
  ban.
- **"No links"** → post the image and caption with the last line removed, and
  put the link in the **first comment**. Nearly every group tolerates that.

## Step 3 · Post, in this order

Never lead with the ask. In a group you are new to, the order is always:

1. **Post 3 (the maths)** — useful, asks for nothing
2. wait 2–3 days
3. **Post 2 (the screenshot)** or **Post 1 (£30)**
4. wait 2–3 days
5. **Post 4 (the tester ask)**

In groups where you already post and people know you, you can start at step 3.

**Spacing across groups.** Never paste the same text into several groups in
the same few minutes — that is the precise pattern Facebook's spam detection
looks for, and the punishment is a posting block on your personal account, not
a deleted post. Leave **at least an hour** between groups, and **change the
first line** each time. The first line is what gets shown in the feed preview,
so varying it helps you anyway.

**Time of day.** For shift workers: **6–8am** (nights coming off, days
travelling in) and **8–10pm**. Avoid mid-afternoon.

## Step 4 · The first hour after posting matters more than the post

Facebook decides how far to push a post largely on what happens in the first
hour. So:

- **Stay on your phone for that hour.** Reply to every single comment, even
  "nice one" — a reply counts as engagement and pulls the post further.
- **Answer disagreement properly.** Someone will say their rates work
  differently, or that their agency does breaks another way. They are usually
  right about their own job. "That's a different setup to mine — you'd set
  yours up as X" is the correct answer, and those threads are where the
  interest comes from.
- **Never argue.** One defensive reply from the person who made the app
  undoes the whole post.
- **Do not delete negative comments** unless they are abusive. A post with an
  argument in it outperforms a post with nothing in it.

## Step 5 · If a post gets removed

It happens and it is not a disaster.

- Removed by an admin → message them once, politely, ask what the issue was,
  do not repost in that group. Do not argue.
- Removed by Facebook → do not repost the same text anywhere for 24 hours.
  Reposting immediately is what turns a removed post into a blocked account.

## Step 6 · Check what worked

Once a week:

```sql
select coalesce(source, 'unknown') as came_from,
       count(*) as signups,
       max(created_at) as latest
from public.launch_signups
group by 1
order by signups desc;
```

That covers waitlist signups by channel. **Tester opt-ins are not in there** —
they are counted in Play Console, and they are the number that decides whether
this fortnight worked.

## What not to do

- **Do not post all four in one group in one week.** It reads as a campaign
  and you lose the group.
- **Do not use a second account** to comment on or like your own post. On
  Facebook it risks both accounts; on Reddit it can get payweek.app banned
  sitewide.
- **Do not buy followers, likes or engagement.** For a product whose whole
  pitch is "no ads, no tracking, honest numbers", being caught is fatal in a
  way it would not be for a normal app.
- **Never say Payweek calculates tax, or that it is legal or financial
  advice.** It estimates gross pay. Your posts have to match what the site
  footer already says.
- **Do not promise a launch date** you do not control. "Waiting on Google" is
  true and nobody minds it.
