# The posts, and exactly how to use them

Seven graphics, seven captions, a three-week rota, and the instructions for
getting them in front of people. `docs/GROWTH.md` is the strategy;
`docs/DESIGN_PHILOSOPHY.md` is why they look the way they do; this is the
doing.

Regenerate the images after any change:

```sh
npx vite --port 5199
node scripts/make-posts.mjs
```

**Every figure is the app's own arithmetic**, from the same fixture as the
screenshots and the film, and every claim has been checked against it:

| Claim | Check |
| --- | --- |
| £50.63 + £60.75 | = £111.38 ✅ |
| £467.53 − £437.53 | = £30.00 ✅ |
| 12.07% of £622.08 | = £75.09 ✅ |
| Mon+Tue+Thu+Fri+Sat | = £622.08 ✅ |

If you change a number in a caption, change it in the graphic too. The
authority of these posts is borrowed entirely from their accuracy — the first
person who adds it up in the comments will be right and you will be wrong.

# Part 1 — the seven posts

## 1 · "Would you have noticed?"
`post-1-would-you-have-noticed.png` · 1080×1350 · **Link:** `payweek.app/?s=fb-30`

The scroll-stopper. Three rows, one red.

> Genuine question for anyone doing agency work.
>
> Do you check your payslip, or do you look at the number and hope?
>
> I stopped hoping after I worked out one of mine was thirty quid light. Four
> shifts, two of them running past midnight — and once you're splitting hours
> across a night rate and taking the break off the band it actually fell in,
> working out what the week *should* have paid is genuinely hard. Hard enough
> that most of us don't. Which is exactly why it goes unnoticed.
>
> So I built something that does it for me. Free, no ads, doesn't track
> anything — I'm not making money off this, I just got sick of not knowing.

## 2 · The night shift
`post-2-night-shift.png` · 1080×1350 · **Link:** `payweek.app/?s=fb-nights`

**Post this first in any group you're new to.** It asks for nothing, which is
what earns the right to post the rest.

> 18:00 to 02:00. Eight hours on the clock, half an hour unpaid. What should
> that pay?
>
> Most people do 8 × their hourly rate and move on. On £13.50 with a £16.20
> night rate from 22:00 that's £108 — and it's wrong. It's £111.38: 3h45 at
> the day rate, 3h45 at nights, break off the band it actually fell in.
>
> Three quid on one shift. Four of those a week is a tank of fuel a month you
> never knew you were owed.
>
> If your rates are different the split is different. The point is that there
> *is* a split, and "hours × rate" is almost never the right answer.

## 4 · Holiday pay
`post-4-holiday-pay.png` · 1080×1350 · **Link:** `payweek.app/?s=fb-holiday`

The most *useful* post in the set. Most agency workers have never had 12.07%
explained to them, and this is the one people tag each other in.

> Does anyone actually know what their holiday pay should be?
>
> 12.07% of everything you earn. Roughly seven minutes of paid holiday for
> every hour you're on the clock. A £622 week accrues about £75.
>
> It builds up whether anyone tells you about it or not, and agencies are not
> famous for volunteering it. The trick is having your own record of the hours,
> because then it's arithmetic rather than an argument.

# Part 2 — the three-week rota

Seven posts is enough to stop repeating yourself, which is what actually gets
you removed. Never the same post in the same group twice.

| Week | Group A (new to you) | Group B (you already post) | TikTok / IG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Mon: **2** (night shift) · Thu: **4** (holiday) | Mon: **1** (£30) · Fri: **3** (tester ask) | 2, 4, 1 |
| 2 | Mon: **1** · Thu: **7** (stats) | Wed: **5** (the week) · Sat: **3** again | 7, 5 |
| 3 | Tue: **5** · Fri: **3** (tester ask) | Tue: **6** (what it doesn't do) | 6, and repost whatever did best |

The order inside a group never changes: **useful first, ask last.**

# Part 3 — instructions

## Step 1 · Find the groups (30 minutes, once)

Facebook search bar → **Groups** tab → search:

- the name of every warehouse or site you've worked at
- the name of every agency you've worked for
- `warehouse jobs <your town>` · `<your town> jobs`
- `agency work uk` · `night shift uk`

Join **8 to 10**, in this priority: site-specific, then agency-specific, then
town jobs groups. A 400-member group for one distribution centre beats a
90,000-member national jobs group, because everyone in the small one is paid
by the same agency.

**The membership questions.** Answer as a worker, because you are one — "I
work agency at <site>" gets you in. Anything that reads like a business gets
you rejected. Don't mention the app yet.

## Step 2 · Read the rules before posting anything

Group → **About** → rules. You're looking for one thing: whether promotion or
links are banned.

- **No rule against it** → post normally.
- **"No promotion" / "No selling"** → message an admin first: *"Hi — I work
  agency myself and I've made a free app for working out shift pay. No ads,
  nothing for sale. Alright if I post about it?"* You get a yes more often
  than you'd think, and a no costs nothing but saves the ban.
- **"No links"** → post image and caption with the link line removed, put the
  link in the **first comment**. Nearly every group tolerates that.

## Step 3 · Spacing

**Never paste the same text into several groups within a few minutes.** That
is the exact pattern Facebook's spam detection looks for, and the punishment
is a posting block on your personal account, not a deleted post. Leave **at
least an hour** between groups and **change the first line** each time — it's
what shows in the feed preview anyway, so varying it helps you.

**Time of day for shift workers:** 6–8am (nights coming off, days travelling
in) and 8–10pm. Avoid mid-afternoon.

## Step 4 · The first hour matters more than the post

Facebook decides how far to push a post largely on early engagement.

- **Stay on your phone for that hour.** Reply to every comment, even "nice
  one" — replies count and pull the post further.
- **Answer disagreement properly.** Someone will say their rates work
  differently. They're right about their own job. "That's a different setup —
  you'd set yours up as X" is the answer, and those threads are where the
  interest comes from.
- **Never argue.** One defensive reply from the person who built it undoes the
  whole post.
- **Don't delete negative comments** unless they're abusive. A post with an
  argument in it beats a post with nothing in it.

## Step 5 · If a post gets removed

- **By an admin** → message once, politely, ask what the issue was, don't
  repost in that group, don't argue.
- **By Facebook** → don't repost the same text anywhere for 24 hours.
  Reposting immediately is what turns a removed post into a blocked account.

## Step 6 · Check what worked, weekly

```sql
select coalesce(source, 'unknown') as came_from,
       count(*) as signups,
       max(created_at) as latest
from public.launch_signups
group by 1
order by signups desc;
```

Waitlist only. **Tester opt-ins are counted in Play Console**, and that is the
number that decides whether the fortnight worked.

## What not to do

- **Don't post all seven in one group in one week.** It reads as a campaign
  and you lose the group.
- **Don't use a second account** to comment on or like your own posts. On
  Facebook it risks both accounts; on Reddit it can get payweek.app banned
  sitewide.
- **Don't buy followers, likes or engagement.** For a product whose pitch is
  "no ads, no tracking, honest numbers", being caught is fatal in a way it
  wouldn't be for a normal app.
- **Never say Payweek calculates tax, or that it is legal or financial
  advice.** It estimates gross pay, and your posts have to match what the site
  footer already says.
- **Don't promise a launch date** you don't control. "Waiting on Google" is
  true and nobody minds it.
